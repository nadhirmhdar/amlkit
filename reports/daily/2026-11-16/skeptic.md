## What the team missed
1. **TD1 and N3c are the same defect class: exact-string logic over uncontrolled input, on the two controls that matter most.** One defeats the AED 55,000 structuring check by changing the case of a field; the other defeats sanctions matching by changing one Arabic letter. Both would be caught by normalising at the model boundary (closed set for `direction`, a fuller letter fold). The loop has now found this three ways (names on 10-04, sanctions on 11-16, structuring on 11-14); an "every free input that feeds a rule" inventory would find the rest.
2. **TX1 and TD1 undermine the same rule from opposite ends:** the amount can be shrunk by a typed AED figure and the grouping can be split by a direction spelling. Together they make the structuring control depend on honest data entry, which is exactly what it exists to check. Nobody tested both in one scenario.
3. **CL1 is the first finding about what the product allows an operator to do next, not what it accepts as data.** It shares a root with MA1 and SO1: lifecycle actions (close, deactivate, switch four-eyes off) have no preconditions tied to open obligations. A short "what must be true before X" table per action would be a natural spec for the next fix wave.
4. **I missed a day.** After reading the 11-14 prompt I did not write a report; two later firings arrived together and the day was lost. The roles' 11-14 reports contained TD1, TD2 and TX1-TX3, which I only adjudicate now, two days late. Findings are not lost, but the severity ratings for them did not exist at the time.
5. **Nine days with master unchanged and the open Medium list growing:** SO1, RL1, MA1, RP1, SS5, CR1, TD1, TX1, CL1, N3c. That is ten items, all with small fixes and clear owners in code. The loop is now producing a backlog faster than anything consumes it.
