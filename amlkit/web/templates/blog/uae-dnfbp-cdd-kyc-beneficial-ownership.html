What CDD actually requires

Customer due diligence is the work of answering three questions about everyone you do business with: who are they, who actually stands behind them, and how much risk does that relationship carry. It is a continuing obligation that starts before you onboard a customer and keeps running for as long as the relationship lasts.

Where this sits relative to sanctions screening Sanctions screening (see our 24-hour rule guide) is a binary check against specific lists, with its own freeze-and-report duty. CDD is everything else: identity, ownership, purpose of the relationship, and the ongoing judgement about how closely to watch it. A customer can pass sanctions screening cleanly and still need enhanced due diligence.

Verifying identity, including who's behind it

For a natural person, that means a valid, unexpired identity document (passport, Emirates ID) and enough supporting detail — nationality, date of birth, address — to be confident you have the right individual. For a legal person or arrangement, identity verification extends to:

  • the entity's constitutional documents, trade licence and registered address;
  • the names of directors, partners and anyone with authority to bind the entity;
  • the ownership and control structure, down to natural persons; and
  • the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship.

Verification means checking that documentation against an independent, reliable source — not simply accepting what the customer hands you.

Beneficial ownership: down to the natural person

This is where most CDD files fall short in practice. A company's shareholder register tells you who owns shares; it doesn't tell you who ultimately benefits or controls, especially once holding companies, trusts or nominee arrangements are involved. Under Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025, a beneficial owner is a natural person who ultimately owns or controls 25% or more of the shares or voting rights, directly or indirectly, or who exercises control by other means.

No one meets the threshold? Say so. If ownership is diffuse enough that no natural person crosses 25%, you fall back to identifying the senior managing official — and you record, explicitly, that no beneficial owner above the threshold could be identified. That statement, dated and reasoned, is the correct outcome in some structures. A blank beneficial-ownership field is not.

Multi-layer and offshore structures need the chain traced all the way through, not just to the first holding company. A customer who can't or won't produce an ownership chain is itself a risk signal, and one that typically pushes the relationship toward enhanced due diligence rather than a shrug.

When CDD is triggered, and the AED thresholds

CDD is always required when you establish a business relationship. Beyond that, Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025 sets specific monetary triggers for occasional (non-relationship) transactions:

TriggerThreshold
Occasional transaction (no ongoing relationship)AED 55,000 or more, single or linked transactions
Wire transferAED 3,500 or more

CDD is also required whenever money laundering or terrorist financing is suspected, or when you doubt the accuracy of identification data already on file — both apply regardless of transaction size.

Simplified, standard or enhanced: the three tiers

CDD is not one fixed procedure. The risk-based approach gives you three tiers, and the tier follows the customer's assessed risk, not just their category:

TierWhen it appliesWhat changes
Simplified (SDD)Lower-risk customer, supported by your own documented risk assessment, with no EDD trigger presentLighter identity verification and longer periodic-review intervals
StandardThe default tier for most customersFull identity and beneficial-ownership verification, ordinary monitoring cadence
Enhanced (EDD)Any EDD trigger is present — see belowSenior-management sign-off, source-of-wealth checks, shorter review cycles, closer transaction monitoring

How a specific customer lands in one tier rather than another is the output of a risk assessment, not a judgement call made on the spot — see our guide to risk assessment and risk scoring for how that works.

What forces enhanced due diligence

Some conditions move a customer to enhanced due diligence regardless of what the rest of the score says:

  • a politically exposed person (PEP), their family member or close associate;
  • a customer or transaction connected to a FATF high-risk or increased-monitoring jurisdiction;
  • a sanctions or Local Terrorist List hit (which also triggers the separate freeze obligation);
  • unexplained complexity in ownership or transaction structure with no clear business rationale; and
  • adverse media alleging financial crime, even absent a conviction.

Source of funds and source of wealth

For higher-risk customers, verifying identity and ownership is not enough — you also need to understand where the money in a specific transaction came from (source of funds) and, for PEPs and other higher-risk relationships, how the customer built their overall wealth (source of wealth). Both need documentary support where reasonably available: payslips, sale agreements, audited accounts, inheritance or gift documentation. An explanation you can't tie to any evidence is a gap in the file, not a completed check.

Ongoing monitoring: CDD doesn't end at onboarding

A CDD file that is accurate on day one and never looked at again is not compliant on day two. Ongoing monitoring means:

  1. Transaction monitoring against the customer's expected profile, flagging activity that doesn't fit;
  2. Periodic reviews at an interval set by risk rating — more frequent for higher-risk customers;
  3. Event-driven re-checks whenever something material changes: new beneficial owners, a change of business activity, a jurisdiction change, an adverse-media hit, or a transaction pattern that no longer makes sense; and
  4. Re-screening against sanctions and PEP lists whenever those lists update, not just at onboarding.

Records to keep

  • Identity documents and the beneficial-ownership chain, including how you verified them.
  • The risk assessment and its dated history, not just the current rating.
  • Source-of-funds/wealth evidence for every higher-risk relationship.
  • Every periodic review and event-driven re-check, with its outcome.

Keep all of it for at least five years after the relationship ends, consistent with the retention duty that runs through UAE AML/CFT law generally.
