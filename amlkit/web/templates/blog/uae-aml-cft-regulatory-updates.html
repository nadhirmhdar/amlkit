The headline change: a new law from the ground up

Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 on anti-money laundering, combating the financing of terrorism and proliferation financing came into effect on 14 October 2025, replacing Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 outright rather than amending it. It's the primary statute every AML/CFT obligation in the UAE now traces back to, including the Executive Regulations below.

At a structural level, the new law expands scope to proliferation financing as its own standalone offence (not just an adjunct to terrorist financing), brings virtual assets and VASPs explicitly within the federal AML framework, and gives the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) a stronger toolkit — covered in more detail below.

The Executive Regulations: the detail that actually governs you

A framework law is only as useful as the regulation that operationalises it. Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025, the Executive Regulations to the new law, took effect 14 December 2025 and is where the operational detail — thresholds, categories, specific duties — actually lives. Day to day, this is the document your compliance program needs to match, not the framework law itself.

What the Executive Regulations are reported to set for DNFBPs

AreaReported in the Executive Regulations
Beneficial ownershipReported as a 25% shareholding/voting-rights threshold, with fallback to the senior managing official (confirm against the Resolution)
CDD trigger thresholdsReported as AED 55,000 for occasional transactions and AED 3,500 for wire transfers (confirm against the Resolution). For some DNFBP sectors, such as dealers in precious metals and stones and real estate, AED 55,000 is also tied to cash-transaction reporting, a separate duty from CDD. Confirm which applies to you
ScopeVirtual asset service providers brought explicitly within the regulated framework, alongside the existing DNFBP categories (real estate, precious metals and stones, corporate service providers, auditors/accountants, lawyers/notaries)

None of this is a wholesale change in philosophy — risk-based CDD, beneficial-ownership identification and suspicion-based reporting were already the model under the 2018 law. The figures in the table are reported as restated in the Executive Regulations; we have not confirmed which of them, if any, are new compared with earlier guidance or the 2018 regime.

Expanded FIU powers: postponement, suspension and freezing

Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 gives the FIU a materially stronger set of tools than the 2018 law did: the power to postpone a suspicious transaction on an urgent basis and to suspend it or freeze funds for limited periods set in the law (secondary sources report 10 working days and 30 days; confirm the exact periods against the Decree-Law text), independent of the separate sanctions-list freezing regime covered in our 24-hour rule guide. If a reporting entity receives a postponement, suspension or freeze instruction from the FIU under this power, that is a direct order to comply with, separate from and in addition to your own STR/SAR filing duty — see our goAML filing guide for how the underlying report gets filed.

A reporting name changed: FFR to CNMR

A smaller but practically important change: the report that used to be called the Funds Freeze Report (FFR) for a confirmed sanctions-list match is now referred to throughout current UAE goAML guidance as the Confirmed Name Match Report (CNMR). The underlying obligation — report a confirmed match within five business days of freezing — hasn't changed; only the name has. If your internal procedures or training materials still say "FFR," update the terminology so staff recognise the current form on goAML.

Country-risk lists keep moving

The Ministry of Economy periodically updates the lists of FATF high-risk and increased-monitoring jurisdictions that feed directly into CDD and enhanced due diligence decisions (see our risk assessment guide for how geographic risk factors into scoring). An update we have seen cited is Circular No. (1) of 2026 (reported as issued 11 March 2026), but we could not re-verify its text, so check the Ministry's website for the current circular. These lists move independently of the main legislative changes above, and a stale country-risk list quietly undermines an otherwise-compliant CDD program.

What this means for your compliance program, practically

  1. Re-point every policy document that still cites Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 or the pre-December-2025 thresholds to the current law and Executive Regulations.
  2. Check beneficial-ownership files against the reported 25% threshold (confirm it against the Resolution), and backfill documentation for structures where that wasn't previously recorded clearly.
  3. Update terminology in training and templates — CNMR, not FFR.
  4. Re-check your high-risk-country list against the latest Ministry of Economy circular, not whatever list you last updated.
  5. Know the FIU's postponement/suspension/freeze powers exist, so an instruction under them doesn't come as a surprise to whoever receives it.
