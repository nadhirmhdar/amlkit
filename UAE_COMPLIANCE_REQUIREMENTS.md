## Legal Framework

### Primary Legislation

**Federal Decree by Law No. 10 of 2025**
- Title: Anti-Money Laundering, and Combating the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation Financing
- Replaces: Federal Decree Law No. 20 of 2018
- Effective: 2025
- Scope: All financial institutions and DNFBPs in UAE

### Money Laundering Definition

Money laundering occurs when a person knows or should reasonably suspect funds are proceeds of crime and intentionally:
- Transfers or moves proceeds to conceal their illegal source
- Conceals or disguises the true nature, source, or location of proceeds
- Acquires, possesses, or uses proceeds upon receipt
- Assists the perpetrator of the predicate offense to escape punishment

**Note:** Money laundering is an independent crime - perpetrators can be punished for both the predicate offense and money laundering separately.

## Core Obligations

### 1. Risk-Based Approach (RBA)

**National Risk Assessment (2024)**
- Latest: 2024 UAE National Risk Assessment published
- Requirement: All entities must understand their risk profile within national context
- Focus areas:
  - Trade-based money laundering
  - Use of third parties and complex legal structures
  - Virtual assets and cybercrime
  - Proliferation financing
  - Terrorism financing

**Business Risk Assessment (BRA)**
- Conduct institutional vulnerability assessment
- Identify ML/TF/PF risks specific to business model
- Review and update regularly (at least annually)
- Document risk assessment methodology

**Customer Risk Assessment (CRA)**
- Evaluate ML/TF risks for each client relationship
- Risk factors: customer type, geography, products/services, delivery channels
- Risk categorization: Low, Medium, High
- Implementation guides available from Ministry of Economy

### 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

**Standard CDD Requirements:**
- Verify customer identity using reliable, independent sources
- Identify and verify beneficial owners (natural persons owning >25% or exercising control)
- Understand the purpose and intended nature of business relationship
- Obtain information on source of funds/wealth for higher-risk customers
- Conduct ongoing monitoring of transactions and relationship

**Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)**
Required for:
- High-risk customers (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures)
- High-risk transactions or business relationships
- Situations where higher risk is identified through CRA
- Non-face-to-face customer relationships

**Simplified Due Diligence (SDD)**
- Available for lower-risk scenarios per Circular No. 6 of 2025
- Must still conduct risk assessment to justify simplified measures
- Not permitted for high-risk categories

**Beneficial Ownership**
- Identify ultimate beneficial owner (UBO)
- Natural person owning/controlling >25% directly or indirectly
- If no person meets threshold, identify senior managing official
- Procedures per Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023
- Verify using official documents or reliable sources

**Ongoing Monitoring**
- Keep CDD information up to date
- Monitor transactions to ensure consistency with customer profile
- Investigate unusual or suspicious patterns
- Enhanced monitoring for high-risk customers

### 3. Record Keeping

**Transaction Records:**
- Maintain records of all transactions (domestic and international)
- Include sufficient detail to permit reconstruction of transaction
- Retention: Minimum 5 years from completion of transaction

**CDD Records:**
- Customer identification documents
- Business correspondence
- Results of analysis (including suspicious transaction reports)
- Retention: Minimum 5 years after termination of relationship

**Availability:**
- Records must be readily available for inspection by supervisory authorities
- Provide records upon request within reasonable timeframe
- Ensure records are in searchable format

### 4. Compliance Officer & Controls

**Compliance Officer Requirements:**
- Appoint designated AML/CFT Compliance Officer at management level
- Sufficient authority, resources, and independence
- Direct reporting line to senior management/board
- Responsibilities:
  - Develop and implement AML/CFT policies and procedures
  - Monitor compliance with regulations
  - Serve as point of contact with FIU and supervisors
  - Oversee employee training programs
  - Prepare and submit reports

**Internal Controls:**
- Establish written AML/CFT policies, controls, and procedures
- Independent audit function to test compliance
- Risk management framework
- Technology systems for transaction monitoring and sanctions screening
- Clear lines of responsibility

**Training:**
- Provide regular AML/CFT training to all relevant employees
- Training must cover:
  - ML/TF/PF typologies and red flags
  - Legal and regulatory requirements
  - Internal policies and procedures
  - STR/SAR reporting obligations
- Document training attendance and materials
- Update training for new developments

### 5. Targeted Financial Sanctions (TFS)

**Screening Obligations:**
- Screen all customers, beneficial owners, and counterparties
- Screen against:
  - UN Security Council sanctions lists
  - UAE local terrorism list
  - Other relevant sanctions lists
- Screening must occur:
  - At onboarding
  - When conducting transactions
  - Periodically for existing customers (at least quarterly)

**Freezing Measures:**
- Immediately freeze assets and funds of designated persons/entities
- Prohibit making funds or assets available to designated persons
- No prior notice or authorization required for freezing
- Freezing must occur without delay upon identification

**Reporting:**
- Report any matches or attempted transactions involving designated persons
- Report to:
  - UAE Central Bank (for financial institutions)
  - Relevant supervisor (for DNFBPs)
  - FIU
- Timeframe: Within 24 hours of identification
- Include full details of frozen assets/transactions

**List Updates:**
- Subscribe to sanctions list update services
- Implement process to incorporate list updates immediately
- Re-screen existing customer base when lists are updated

## DNFBP Requirements

### Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions

**Four Sectors Supervised by Ministry of Economy:**

#### 1. Real Estate Agents & Brokers

**Covered Activities:**
- Buying and selling real estate
- Managing real estate
- Other real estate transactions above AED 55,000

**Key Requirements:**
- goAML registration mandatory
- Real Estate Activity Reports required
- CDD for all property transactions
- Special attention to:
  - Cash transactions
  - Purchases by non-residents
  - Complex ownership structures
  - Rapid buying/selling patterns

**Red Flags:**
- Transaction price significantly differs from market value
- Frequent property flipping without clear economic rationale
- Use of nominee arrangements
- Payment structures involving multiple jurisdictions

#### 2. Dealers in Precious Metals & Stones

**Covered Activities:**
- Dealing in precious metals (gold, silver, platinum, palladium)
- Dealing in precious stones (diamonds, rubies, sapphires, emeralds)
- Cash transactions above AED 55,000

**Key Requirements:**
- goAML registration mandatory
- Activity reports via goAML (Circular No. 5/2022)
- Due diligence on sourcing per Ministerial Decree No. 68 of 2024 (for gold refineries)
- Enhanced scrutiny for:
  - Large cash purchases
  - Multiple smaller transactions below reporting threshold
  - Unusual purchase patterns

**Red Flags:**
- Customer requests unusual payment arrangements
- Lack of interest in quality/authenticity (focus only on price)
- Requests for melting/destruction of items that could be resold
- Rapid turnover of inventory

#### 3. Trust & Company Service Providers

**Covered Activities:**
- Acting as formation agent for legal persons
- Acting or arranging for another person to act as director/secretary of company
- Providing registered office, business address, correspondence/administrative address
- Acting or arranging for another person to act as trustee
- Acting or arranging for another person to act as nominee shareholder

**Key Requirements:**
- goAML registration mandatory
- Enhanced CDD for complex structures
- Understand and document actual business activities
- Identify and verify all beneficial owners
- Ongoing monitoring of client structures

**Red Flags:**
- Unjustified complexity in corporate structure
- Use of multiple jurisdictions without economic rationale
- Nominee arrangements obscuring true ownership
- Reluctance to provide beneficial ownership information
- Frequent changes in corporate structure or management

#### 4. Independent Accountants & Auditors

**Covered Activities:**
- Financial audits
- Tax advisory services
- Other accounting/bookkeeping services above AED 55,000

**Key Requirements:**
- goAML registration mandatory
- Professional skepticism in client engagements
- CDD integrated with client acceptance procedures
- Awareness of ML/TF risks in financial reporting
- Report suspicious patterns in client financial activities

**Red Flags:**
- Unexplained transactions in client books
- Complex offshore arrangements without business rationale
- Inconsistencies between stated business and transactions
- Reluctance to provide documentation
- Frequent changes in accountants/auditors

## Reporting Requirements

### Suspicious Transaction/Activity Reports (STR/SAR)

**When to Report:**
- When you know, suspect, or have reasonable grounds to suspect:
  - Funds are proceeds of criminal activity
  - Funds are related to terrorism or terrorism financing
  - Transaction has no apparent economic or lawful purpose
  - Transaction is structured to avoid reporting requirements

**No Minimum Threshold:**
- Report all suspicious activities regardless of amount
- Multiple small transactions may be as suspicious as large ones
- Pattern of behavior is key indicator

**Reporting System:**
- **goAML** - Unified reporting platform developed by UNODC
- UAE is first Gulf country to implement goAML
- Online portal: https://www.goaml.uaefi u.gov.ae
- Secure, encrypted submission
- Confirmation receipt provided

**What to Include in STR/SAR:**
- Customer identification information
- Description of suspicious activity
- Reason for suspicion (specific indicators)
- Transaction details (amounts, dates, accounts, parties)
- Supporting documentation
- Any other relevant information

**Timeframe:**
- Report promptly upon identification of suspicious activity
- No explicit deadline specified, but "without delay" is expected
- Best practice: Within 24-48 hours of forming suspicion

**Tipping Off Prohibition:**
- Do NOT inform customer that STR/SAR has been filed
- Do NOT inform anyone else not directly involved in compliance function
- Violation: Criminal offense with penalties

**Legal Protection:**
- Good faith reporting is protected by law
- No civil or criminal liability for filing STR/SAR in good faith
- Employment protection - cannot be terminated for filing report

### Sector-Specific Activity Reports

**Dealers in Precious Metals & Stones:**
- Activity reports via goAML (Circular No. 5/2022)
- Report volumes, transaction patterns, high-value sales
- Helps FIU identify sector-wide trends

**Real Estate:**
- Real Estate Activity Reports required
- Report property transactions above threshold
- Include details of parties, amounts, financing arrangements

### International Transfer Reports

**Requirements under FATF Recommendation 16:**
- Financial institutions must include complete originator and beneficiary information
- Cross-border wire transfers must include:
  - Originator name and account number
  - Originator address or national ID or date/place of birth
  - Beneficiary name and account number
- Domestic transfers: Less stringent (account number sufficient unless suspicious)

### Cash Transaction Reports (CTRs)

**Threshold:**
- AED 55,000 or equivalent in foreign currency
- Single transaction or multiple linked transactions

**Reporting:**
- To FIU via goAML
- Within specified timeframe (typically 15 days)
- Include full customer identification and transaction details

## Targeted Financial Sanctions

### UN Security Council Sanctions Lists

**Applicable UN Sanctions Regimes:**
- **UNSCR 1267/1989/2253** - ISIL (Da'esh) and Al-Qaida sanctions
- **UNSCR 1988** - Taliban sanctions
- **UNSCR 1718/2087/2094/2270/2321/2371/2375/2397** - Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK) sanctions
- **UNSCR 1737** - Iran sanctions (re-imposed per Circular 7/2025)
- Other Chapter VII resolutions as applicable

**Implementation in UAE:**
- Cabinet Resolution No. 74 of 2020 governs TFS implementation
- Automatic incorporation - no separate UAE legislation needed for UN lists
- Updates effective immediately upon UN publication

### UAE Local Terrorism List

**Designated Groups:**
- List maintained by UAE authorities
- Includes organizations designated by UAE government as terrorist
- Updates published via official channels

**Access:**
- Available through Central Bank website
- Distributed to supervised entities
- Updates via email/notification to registered entities

### High-Risk Jurisdictions

**FATF Lists:**
Ministry of Economy updates UAE position via circulars based on FATF determinations:

**Call for Action Jurisdictions (Blacklist):**
- Strictest countermeasures required
- Enhanced due diligence mandatory
- Consider additional restrictions on business relationships
- Recent updates: Circulars No. 1/2026, 8/2025

**Increased Monitoring Jurisdictions (Greylist):**
- Enhanced scrutiny required
- Proportionate risk mitigation measures
- Monitor developments and reassess risk regularly
- Recent updates: Circulars No. 5/2025, 1/2025, 4/2024

**Current Lists:**
- Circular No. 1/2026 (latest update)
- Entities must check FATF website for most current list
- Implement measures promptly upon FATF announcements

### Screening Obligations

**Frequency:**
- **At onboarding** - All new customers and beneficial owners
- **Transaction time** - Before processing transactions
- **Periodic screening** - At least quarterly for existing customers
- **Upon list updates** - Re-screen entire customer base when lists change

**Scope:**
Screen against:
- UN Consolidated Sanctions List
- UAE Local Terrorism List
- OFAC SDN List (if dealing with USD transactions)
- EU Sanctions Lists (if relevant to business)
- Other applicable jurisdictions

**Technology:**
- Automated screening systems recommended
- Fuzzy matching algorithms to catch name variations
- Regular system updates with new list versions
- Document screening results and match decisions

### Freezing and Reporting

**Immediate Freezing:**
- Freeze assets without delay upon positive match
- No requirement to await confirmation from authorities
- Prohibition includes:
  - Making funds available to designated person
  - Making funds available for benefit of designated person
  - Providing financial services

**Asset Types:**
- Cash and bank accounts
- Securities and investments
- Real property
- Precious metals/stones
- Virtual assets
- Any other economic resources

**Reporting to Authorities:**

Must report within 24 hours to:
1. **Central Bank of UAE** (for financial institutions):
   - Email: amlcft@centralbank.ae
   - Include full details of frozen assets
   - Customer information
   - Attempted transactions

2. **Ministry of Economy** (for DNFBPs):
   - Via official channels
   - Include comprehensive details

3. **Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)**:
   - Via goAML system
   - Tag report as TFS-related

**Information to Include:**
- Full name of designated person/entity
- Identification documents/registration numbers
- Description of frozen assets (type, amount, location)
- Details of attempted transaction (if applicable)
- Relationship to your entity (customer, beneficiary, etc.)

### Exemptions and Licenses

**Humanitarian Exemptions:**
- May apply to authorities for license to unfreeze funds for:
  - Basic expenses (food, rent, medicine)
  - Legal fees
  - Extraordinary expenses
- Application must demonstrate necessity
- Approval required before releasing funds

**Authorization Process:**
- Submit request to Central Bank (for FIs) or relevant supervisor
- Include detailed justification
- Await written approval before proceeding
- Maintain records of authorization

### False Positive Handling

**When Screening Generates Possible Match:**
1. Conduct enhanced review of match quality
2. Compare identifying information (name, DOB, passport, address)
3. Document analysis and decision
4. If not a true match:
   - Document reasons for determination
   - Retain records of analysis
   - Proceed with transaction
5. If uncertain:
   - Report to authorities for guidance
   - Do not proceed with transaction until clarified

### Training on TFS

**Required Training Elements:**
- Understanding of applicable sanctions regimes
- Screening procedures and technology use
- Freezing obligations and procedures
- Reporting requirements and timeframes
- Consequences of non-compliance
- Regular updates on new designations

## Penalties & Enforcement

### Administrative Penalties

**Framework:**
- Cabinet Decision No. 132 of 2023 - General violations and penalty framework
- Cabinet Resolution No. 71 of 2024 - MOE and Ministry of Justice supervised entities

**Common Violations and Penalties:**

| Violation | Penalty Range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Failure to implement adequate CDD | AED 50,000 - 500,000 |
| Non-reporting of suspicious transactions | AED 100,000 - 500,000 |
| Inadequate record-keeping | AED 50,000 - 300,000 |
| Non-compliance with TFS requirements | AED 100,000 - 1,000,000 |
| Failure to register in goAML | AED 20,000 - 100,000 |
| Tipping off | AED 50,000 - 500,000 |
| Inadequate internal controls | AED 50,000 - 500,000 |
| Failure to appoint compliance officer | AED 50,000 - 200,000 |
| Insufficient employee training | AED 30,000 - 150,000 |

**Aggravating Factors:**
- Repeated violations
- Large-scale or systematic non-compliance
- Involvement of senior management
- Actual ML/TF facilitation (not just control failure)

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Self-reporting of violations
- Remediation efforts undertaken
- Cooperation with supervisors
- First-time violation with minor impact

### Criminal Penalties

**Money Laundering Offenses:**
Under Federal Decree Law No. 10 of 2025:
- **Imprisonment:** Up to 10 years
- **Fines:** Not less than AED 100,000
- **Asset confiscation:** Proceeds of crime and instrumentalities
- **Business suspension or closure:** For corporate offenders

**Terrorism Financing:**
- **Imprisonment:** Life imprisonment or minimum 10 years
- **Fines:** Not less than AED 100,000
- **Asset confiscation:** All assets used or intended for terrorism financing
- **Loss of civil and political rights**

**Proliferation Financing:**
- **Imprisonment:** Up to 10 years
- **Fines:** Not less than AED 100,000
- **Asset confiscation:** Assets related to proliferation activities

**Tipping Off:**
- **Imprisonment:** Up to 2 years
- **Fines:** Not less than AED 100,000

### Supervisory Actions

**Ministry of Economy Enforcement Tools:**

1. **Warning Letters**
   - Formal notice of deficiency
   - Requirement to remediate within specified timeframe
   - Follow-up inspection to verify compliance

2. **Supervisory Directions**
   - Binding requirements to implement specific measures
   - May include:
     - Enhance internal controls
     - Conduct independent audit
     - Provide additional training
     - Limit certain activities

3. **Administrative Penalties**
   - Monetary fines as per penalty framework
   - Published on MOE website (for serious violations)
   - Payment deadline typically 30 days

4. **License Suspension**
   - Temporary suspension of business license
   - Required for serious or repeated violations
   - May require remediation plan before reinstatement

5. **License Revocation**
   - Permanent removal of authorization to operate
   - Reserved for most serious violations
   - Right to appeal to judicial authorities

**Central Bank Enforcement Tools:**

1. **Compliance Orders**
   - Specific actions required to address deficiencies
   - Timebound deadlines

2. **Administrative Fines**
   - Per CBUAE regulations and penalty framework
   - Can be substantial for licensed financial institutions

3. **Restrictions on Business**
   - Limit certain activities (e.g., onboarding new customers)
   - Prohibition on dividend distribution
   - Restrictions on management appointments

4. **License Suspension or Revocation**
   - Temporary or permanent loss of banking/financial license
   - Rare but possible for egregious violations

### Recent Enforcement Actions

**Public Disclosures:**
- MOE publishes enforcement actions on website
- Increases transparency and deters violations
- Names of violators and nature of violations disclosed
- Penalty amounts publicized

**Trends:**
Recent enforcement has focused on:

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: What is the threshold for suspicious transaction reporting?**
A: There is no minimum threshold. Report any transaction or pattern of transactions that you know, suspect, or have reasonable grounds to suspect involves money laundering, terrorism financing, or proliferation financing, regardless of amount.

**Q2: How quickly must I report a suspicious transaction?**
A: While there is no explicit deadline, reports should be submitted "without delay" upon forming suspicion. Best practice is within 24-48 hours of identification.

**Q3: Do I need to wait for a transaction to complete before reporting?**
A: No. You should report attempted transactions and suspicious activity even if no transaction took place. Report the suspicious behavior or pattern.

**Q4: Can I continue doing business with a customer after filing an STR/SAR?**
A: Yes, unless instructed otherwise by the FIU or your supervisor, you may continue the business relationship. However, you must not tip off the customer that a report has been filed.

**Q5: What if I file an STR/SAR but later determine it was not actually suspicious?**
A: Good faith reporting is protected by law. There is no penalty for filing a report that turns out to be a false positive. It is better to report when in doubt.

**Q6: How long do I have to freeze assets after identifying a sanctions match?**
A: Immediately, without delay. Freezing must occur upon identification of a designated person. Then report to authorities within 24 hours.

**Q7: What if my sanctions screening generates a possible match but I'm not certain it's the same person?**
A: Conduct enhanced review comparing identifying information (name, DOB, passport, address). Document your analysis. If you cannot confidently rule out the match, report to authorities for guidance and do not proceed with the transaction.

**Q8: How often must I update my risk assessment?**
A: Business Risk Assessment (BRA) should be reviewed at least annually and whenever there are material changes to your business. Customer Risk Assessments (CRA) should be updated upon significant events (e.g., unusual transaction, change in beneficial ownership, relocation to high-risk jurisdiction) and periodically based on risk level.

**Q9: What are the consequences of non-compliance?**
A: Consequences can include: administrative penalties (AED 20,000 to 1,000,000+ depending on violation), business license suspension or revocation, public disclosure of violations, and criminal penalties for serious offenses (imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation).

**Q10: Where can I get help with compliance?**
A: Contact your supervisor:
- DNFBPs: Ministry of Economy at 800 1222 or WhatsApp +971 800 1222
- Financial Institutions: Central Bank of UAE
- Access supplemental guidance, implementation guides, and video tutorials on respective regulator websites
- Consider professional training through International Compliance Association (ICA)
