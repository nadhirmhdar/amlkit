# Global Consultants in UAE AML Compliance — and where a tool fits

Requested comparison against global consultancies operating in UAE AML
services. The finding changes the positioning, so it is worth stating up front.

---

## The number that reframes everything

| Route | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Outsourced MLRO (UAE market rate)** | **AED 8,000 – 30,000 / month** → AED 96k–360k/yr (~$26k–98k) |
| **In-house MLRO (full-time hire)** | **USD 120,000 – 250,000 / year** |
| Self-serve screening software | $152 – $3,650 / year |
| Enterprise AML platform | $25,000 – $200,000+ / year |

**Screening software is not the expensive part of UAE AML compliance. People
are.** An outsourced MLRO costs 7–170× more than the software they operate.

This inverts the product thesis from the pricing teardown. Undercutting
ComplyAdvantage on licence fees attacks a line item that is already small.
The larger prize is making the *human* hours cheaper — which is a tooling
problem, and one nobody is solving for this segment.

---

## The landscape

### Tier 1 — Big Four
**Deloitte · KPMG · EY · PwC** — all four run financial-crime practices in the
UAE covering AML programme design, model validation, regulatory remediation
and managed services. KPMG is rated top-tier for Risk & Compliance in the
Middle East; all are investing heavily in AI-backed AML tooling.

- **Clients:** banks, exchanges, large FIs, regulator-mandated remediation
- **Pricing:** unpublished, project-based, six figures typical
- **Not competing for DNFBPs.** A gold trader with 200 clients is below their
  minimum engagement size.

### Tier 2 — Mid-tier global
**Grant Thornton · BDO · Crowe · RSM · Ecovis** — the layer that actually
serves UAE mid-market. Ecovis JRB explicitly markets outsourced MLRO for DIFC
and ADGM. Sell AML health checks, policy drafting, goAML registration,
training, and outsourced MLRO/deputy-MLRO.

- **Pricing:** the AED 8k–30k/month band sits here
- **This is the tier a tool-enabled practice competes with — or joins**

### Tier 3 — Specialist financial-crime firms
**Kroll · FTI Consulting · Alvarez & Marsal · K2 Integrity · Control Risks** —
investigations, EDD reports, forensic tracing, sanctions advisory. Engaged for
specific incidents rather than ongoing compliance. Day rates.

### Tier 4 — UAE-local compliance boutiques
**AML UAE · HHMC · Corpin · Adilzone · Racora · Sky Sigma** and dozens more.
Bundled AML + ESR + accounting + audit packages, entry points advertised from
**AED 4,500**. Deploy a working AML programme — policy, controls, goAML
registration, training — in **4–6 weeks**.

- **Highest volume, thinnest margins, most price transparency**
- Largely running on spreadsheets and manual screening

## What consultants actually do that software does not

| Activity | Software | Consultant |
|---|---|---|
| Screen names against lists | ✅ | — |
| Risk-rate customers | ✅ | ◐ |
| Maintain audit trail | ✅ | — |
| **Act as the MLRO** (a named, accountable person) | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Decide** whether a hit is a true match | ◐ assists | ✅ |
| **Decide** whether to file an STR | ❌ | ✅ |
| Draft AML policy to the firm's business risk | ❌ | ✅ |
| Face the regulator at inspection | ❌ | ✅ |
| Train staff | ❌ | ✅ |

The law requires a **named MLRO** — a human who carries personal liability.
No software substitutes for that, and any product claiming otherwise is
selling a compliance failure. The realistic ceiling for a tool is making that
person faster and their evidence better.

---

## Where this tool actually fits

Three viable positions, in increasing order of value:

**1. Sell software to DNFBPs.** Competes at $152–$3,650/yr against KYC2020 and
NameScan. Real but small, and the entry-floor advantage is the main edge.

**2. Sell software to the consultants.** Tiers 2 and 4 run outsourced MLRO
services across dozens of client books, largely on spreadsheets. A tool that
screens every client of every client, keeps per-entity audit trails, and flags
24-hour breaches is directly margin-accretive to them. Better economics than
selling to end-DNFBPs, and a far shorter sales cycle than Tier 1.

**3. Use it to deliver the service yourself.** Given your practice already
does UAE tax and compliance work, the tool converts an outsourced-MLRO
offering from labour-bound to leveraged. AED 8k–30k/month is the prevailing
rate; the marginal cost of screening one more client on self-hosted free data
is close to zero. **This is where the tool is worth most — as an internal
margin lever, not a product.**

Position 3 also resolves the licensing constraint cleanly. Using OpenSanctions
to deliver a paid service is commercial use and requires a licence — so the
primary-source adapters must be in place first either way. But it removes the
need to build billing, onboarding, support and SOC 2, which is where most of
the cost of position 1 or 2 actually sits.

## What the incumbents are weak at, specifically

1. **Arabic name matching** — the technical gap, already demonstrated.
2. **Nobody serves the small end well.** Big Four won't; boutiques do it
   manually. A 40-client broker is too small for Tier 1–3 and gets spreadsheet
   quality from Tier 4.
3. **Evidence quality.** Consultant deliverables are documents. What survives
   an inspection is a *system of record* — timestamped screenings including
   clear results, immutable audit log, explainable alert scores. That is
   exactly what has been built, and it is unusual at this end of the market.
4. **The 24-hour rule is widely under-served.** Re-screening an entire client
   book within 24 hours of every list update is not manually feasible at scale,
   yet it is a hard requirement. Manual processes silently fail it.

Point 4 is the strongest wedge: a defensible, checkable claim that a manual
competitor structurally cannot make.
